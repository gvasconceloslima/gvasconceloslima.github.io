Doctoral Research

State Capacity, Judicial Policy Implementation & Repeat-Player Litigation

Political Science · University of Lisbon, ISCSP · Ongoing

My doctoral research investigates the mechanisms through which repeat litigants in the financial sector may shape the mobilization of state capacities in the local implementation of Brazil’s national judicial policy on appropriate dispute resolution.

The study focuses on the Court of Justice of Ceará, examining how financial institutions’ information, choices and patterns of participation enter organizational decisions concerning policy implementation.

Research design & analytical approach

The research is designed as an explanatory case study, combining qualitative inquiry with a supporting descriptive quantitative component. Its sources include institutional documents, administrative records and semi-structured interviews with court personnel.

The analysis distinguishes the resources and capacities available to the court from those mobilized in specific implementation decisions. It investigates whether and how interactions with repeat litigants expand, constrain or redirect that mobilization, while considering alternative explanations.

Teaching

My teaching connects academic knowledge with the decisions and challenges students and professionals encounter in research and practice. I teach research methods and academic writing, negotiation and conflict management, innovation and creative problem-solving, and access to justice and emerging technologies.

My documented postgraduate teaching portfolio includes 80+ course offerings and over 1,100 teaching hours across these four areas, reaching professionals from law, public management, business, finance, marketing, engineering, technology and other fields. This record forms part of a broader teaching experience spanning undergraduate and postgraduate education, workshops for companies and public institutions, and invited talks for courts.

I design learning experiences grounded in research, helping participants build a conceptual foundation, examine ideas critically and apply knowledge to real problems. I combine cases, simulations and project work with structured feedback, adapting content and language to each audience.

I also design learning experiences beyond the classroom, including professional study tours in São Paulo and preparation for mediation and negotiation competitions. These activities connect academic learning with professional settings and create opportunities for students to practice, reflect and refine their decisions.

Teaching Areas

I have taught these subjects to diverse audiences through undergraduate and postgraduate courses, company training and courses for public institutions, including the Public Prosecution Service (Ministério Público), and the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB).

Research Methods & Academic Writing

Since 2021, I have taught 60+ postgraduate course offerings in research methods and academic writing, delivering over 880 teaching hours and accounting for an estimated 1,000+ student enrollments. I adapt research methods to multiple fields, including law, public management, business, marketing and project management, connecting shared methodological foundations with participants’ different research interests and professional experiences.

I guide learners from a broad topic to a research question, objectives and a feasible methodological design. My teaching connects qualitative and quantitative approaches, case studies, experience reports and systematic literature reviews with the development of research projects.

Activities use real articles, examples and research-design exercises. Academic writing, citation practices, research integrity and the responsible use of generative AI are integrated into this process, helping participants make informed decisions about both the design and communication of their research.

Negotiation & Conflict Management

In recent years, I have taught negotiation and conflict management through 14 course offering cycles, with over 500 course participations and at least 156 teaching hours. Over the years, these courses have brought together professionals from law, public management, business, finance, marketing, engineering, architecture and technology, allowing participants to examine how negotiation tools apply across different professional settings.

I use cases, role-play and simulations to help students prepare for negotiation and analyze both sides of a conflict. Core topics include positions and interests, options, objective criteria, alternatives to agreement, communication, emotions and process design.

My aim is to help participants develop a structured approach to difficult conversations while remaining attentive to the people involved. Preparation, active listening and perspective-taking become practical tools for understanding disagreement, exploring possibilities and making decisions.

Innovation & Creative Problem-Solving

My teaching in innovation and creative problem-solving has reached more than 50 postgraduate participants from fields including business, marketing, engineering, energy, architecture and operations. This interdisciplinary setting brings different professional experiences into the learning process, creating opportunities to examine a shared problem from multiple perspectives.

I teach creativity as a process that can be developed through problem framing, divergent thinking, connections across fields and the assessment of possible solutions. I regularly use group activities in which participants work through concrete problems, combining brainstorming, active listening, feasibility analysis and implementation planning.

The learning process moves from understanding a problem to developing and testing possible responses. I encourage participants to question assumptions, draw on each other’s experience and explain how their proposed solutions could work in practice.

Access to Justice & Emerging Technologies

I have taught access to justice and emerging technologies across five postgraduate cohorts, connecting debates about technological change with the institutional and social conditions that shape access to justice.

My teaching examines how digital technologies can expand access and create new barriers. Learners analyze online dispute resolution, virtual hearings and artificial intelligence initiatives in the judiciary in relation to inclusion, transparency, efficiency and implementation risks.

Activities combine literature analysis, mapping and benchmarking of institutional initiatives, and the design of possible responses to access barriers. Examples include the use of AI in judicial proceedings, data transparency initiatives and the adoption of jurimetrics. The starting point is the justice problem and the needs of users.

Research Supervision

My supervision experience includes 14 completed final research projects in postgraduate specialization programs, covering mediation and conflict management, access to justice, family law, legal education and technology in the justice system. I support students in formulating research questions, developing feasible research designs and connecting their work to institutional and professional problems.

One example is Hannah Soares Sales de Oliveira’s research on virtual hearings and access to justice, which developed from a postgraduate final research project under my supervision into a co-authored peer-reviewed article in Revista Direito, Estado e Sociedade.

Learning beyond the Classroom

Undergraduate Legal InnovationEngenharia Jurídica · 2023–2024

Between 2023 and 2024, I taught five undergraduate classes of Engenharia Jurídica (Legal Innovation) across three semesters.

I used the film Her to open a conversation about relationships between people and technology. Released a decade earlier, the film offered a way to examine questions that had become immediate with the arrival of generative AI: what legal or social problem were we trying to solve, for whom, and what responsibilities came with using technology?

That question also guided the course’s applied work. Students developed projects addressing social and access-to-justice challenges, moving through problem definition, ideation, the Project Model Canvas, low-fidelity prototypes and presentations to a faculty panel. Ethical uses of AI, algorithmic discrimination and the needs of users formed part of the discussion.

For me, teaching legal innovation means helping students assess how technology can serve people and justify the decisions behind a proposed solution.

Mediation & Negotiation Competition CoachingCAMARB, 2025 · CPR International Mediation Competition, 2026 · IBA–VIAC CDRC Vienna, 2026

I coached a student team through a progressive competition pathway: CAMARB, the CPR International Mediation Competition and IBA–VIAC CDRC Vienna. Preparation included workshops, case analysis, written statements, simulations and the development of mediation and negotiation strategies. At CAMARB 2025, the team’s written mediation plan received third place in the Respondent’s Mediation Plan category.

During the CDRC mediation competition in Vienna, the most important learning often happened between rounds. We would leave a session with feedback from the evaluators, identify what needed to change, and prepare the student mediator to test a different approach in the next one.

Working in an international setting, with teams from Prague, India, Vienna and Spain, created opportunities to consider how culture shapes communication and mediation, and to broaden students’ understanding of professional practice across contexts.

These experiences reinforced my teaching philosophy: professional judgment develops through action, feedback, reflection and another opportunity to act. I believe this cycle also helps students build the courage and resilience needed to navigate uncertainty and face the challenges of professional life.

Professional Immersion in São PauloTwo professional study tours · 2024 and 2025

I believe classroom learning becomes richer when students encounter professional practice and discuss it with the people making decisions in real organizations. With that purpose, I designed two professional study tours in São Paulo, connecting legal education with institutional visits and conversations with practitioners.

The 2024 mission brought undergraduate and postgraduate students into contact with settings including Mercado Livre, Cubo Itaú, Mattos Filho and the São Paulo State Court of Justice. The program explored data-oriented legal departments, visual communication, innovation and different forms of legal practice.

In 2025, I organized a professional immersion program around CAMARB, with visits to Nubank, Mandaliti Advogados and Ópice Blum. Conversations addressed conflict prevention and resolution, technology, privacy and the management of high-volume litigation.

Across both programs, my aim was to bring students’ classroom questions into dialogue with professional partners, creating opportunities to compare approaches, question assumptions and connect concepts with the decisions made in practice.

Conflict Resolution & Institutional Practice

OAB/CE & OAB ConciliaVice-President, Special Commission on Mediation and Conciliation · Since 2022Connecting research, professional education and institutional infrastructure to promote consensual dispute resolution.

At the Ceará section of the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB/CE), I contributed to the design and implementation of OAB Concilia. The initial proposal focused on establishing a Mediation Chamber. Drawing on research on legal education, consensual dispute resolution and the adoption of new professional practices, I proposed a broader architecture combining education for consensus, spaces for dialogue and strategic connections.

My contribution included conceiving this three-pillar structure, co-drafting the Chamber’s governance and procedural documents and fee structure, co-authoring a good-practices guide, and participating in professional education and joint initiatives across thematic commissions. I also wrote and submitted the program’s application to the Brazilian National Council of Justice’s Conciliar é Legal 2024 award, which OAB Concilia received.

Institutional records through 2024 reported 2,930 professionals reached through training, 36,000 lawyers reached through the good-practices guide, 17 thematic commissions involved in joint initiatives, and 30 lawyers trained as mediators and conciliators. The Mediation and Conciliation Chamber was formally inaugurated in April 2024. These are collective results of the program and the Commission’s work.

My broader work in the Commission includes developing and refining the Chamber’s procedures, documents and operational workflows. I also hold an alternate representative position for OAB/CE on Ceará’s Interinstitutional Council on Mediation, Restorative Justice and Peace Culture.

The programs I managed generally lasted 18–24 months and awarded a specialization certificate. They constitute a distinct postgraduate pathway from academic and professional master’s and doctoral degree programs (stricto sensu).

I established regular team and individual meetings, shared planning processes and more consistent communication with students, creating a structure that supported collaboration across programs.

My approach to leadership is to build a team around a shared purpose, clear responsibilities and opportunities to contribute. I value an environment where people can exchange perspectives, raise questions and develop solutions together. Dialogue with students, faculty, university leadership and external partners helps connect academic decisions with the needs and experiences of the people involved.

I also worked to keep the portfolio responsive to changes in legal practice and the professional landscape. This included introducing a Compliance specialization, developing programs at the intersection of law and technology, and creating customized educational offerings for public institutions. My aim was to anticipate emerging professional needs and translate them into academically coherent programs, bringing together relevant expertise, new themes and opportunities for practical application.

Specialization Program DesignProgram Coordinator · University of Fortaleza · 2019–February 2025Curriculum architecture, faculty recruitment and continuous renewal across five specialization lines.

My work in specialization program design connects curriculum development, faculty selection and relationships with students. I begin with the knowledge and capabilities professionals need, then organize the program into a coherent learning pathway that can evolve with changes in law, technology and professional practice.

In Law, Technology and Innovation, I developed an integrated curriculum connecting a common foundation with two professional pathways: Digital Law and Startups, and Data Protection and Digital Governance. This structure brought related areas of knowledge into dialogue while preserving the specific competencies required in each field.

I also conceived the Mediation and Conflict Management specialization from the ground up. The curriculum incorporated Online Dispute Resolution, Dispute System Design and professional skills such as communication and storytelling, preparing participants to understand conflicts, navigate different settings and design appropriate responses.

A central part of my coordination was identifying academics and practitioners whose expertise could make a meaningful contribution to each area of the curriculum. I recruited faculty from Brazil and abroad, cultivated professional relationships and worked with instructors to connect their contributions to the program’s learning objectives.

I maintained direct communication with students and faculty throughout successive cohorts, using their questions and course evaluations to inform curriculum review and adjustments. These relationships helped keep the programs connected to participants’ professional challenges and to developments in each field.

My coordination also included Family and Succession Law, where I revised the curriculum to integrate legal knowledge with conflict management, communication and other professional competencies.

Research Development

Research Support & Capacity-BuildingCoordinator, Law Research and Innovation Unit (NIP-CCJ) · University of Fortaleza · 2022–February 2025Building practical connections between central research administration, faculty and undergraduate researchers.

The challenge was to bring the university’s research-support initiatives closer to faculty and students in their everyday work. Faculty perceived a distance between central research administration and their academic units, creating a need for more accessible guidance on funding opportunities, institutional programs and research development.

The Research and Innovation Units (NIPs) were established to bridge this gap. They connected central research administration with faculty and students, translating opportunities and institutional procedures into practical support while bringing researchers’ questions and needs into university-level discussions.

As part of the founding team, I helped develop the regulations, calls and operating routines for this new structure. As coordinator of the Law unit, I advised faculty on research and innovation submissions, supported the creation of research groups in the CNPq Research Groups Directory, and helped researchers establish Google Scholar profiles. I combined individual assistance with reusable guidance, turning recurring questions into resources that faculty could consult independently.

I also helped design and deliver an annual training pathway for undergraduate researchers, responding to supervisors’ concerns about students entering projects without foundational research skills. The program covered research design, literature review, scientific writing, presentations and academic publishing. I taught the scientific-presentation module and later developed its recorded lessons and materials.

During this period, the team delivered three training cycles and began transitioning the pathway toward asynchronous learning to accommodate students’ classes and internship commitments. By the time I left, the first stage had been converted to online delivery.

These contributions helped establish a research-support structure within the academic unit, produced practical resources for faculty and created a recurring training pathway for students.

Social Impact & International Engagement

CGI U PartnershipInstitutional Liaison · University of Fortaleza / Clinton Global Initiative University · Approximately 2017–2020Helping students develop social-impact proposals and access an international program.

After participating in Clinton Global Initiative University (CGI U) in 2016, I became the university’s operational liaison for the partnership. I promoted the opportunity, organized presentations, advised students on their projects and applications, and maintained direct contact with the CGI U team.

During this period, I advised on approximately 90 submissions, around 40 of which were accepted for participation in CGI U events. I supported selected students before their participation and followed up with them afterward. My role focused on making the partnership accessible and helping students turn social-impact ideas into proposals they could present internationally.
